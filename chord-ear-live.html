Getting a clean read

  • One instrument, close to the mic. Drums, vocals and backing tracks blur the notes.
  • Let chords ring. Strummed or sustained chords read best; fast single-note runs come out as passing chords.
  • Single notes show as notes. One note at a time comes up as its name and octave instead of a chord. E2 is the low E string, E4 the high one.
  • Same notes, different names. C6 and Am7 share every note, so Chord Ear goes by the lowest note to pick the name. Slash chords like C/E show a bass note other than the root.
  • No sound on an iPhone? Turn off silent mode. The chart follows along either way.
  • Tuning drift is fine. It measures how far your instrument sits from A440 and adjusts.

Reference notes · standard tuning

Tap a string to hear its note, then pluck yours along with it. Close to pitch you'll hear a slow wobble between the two; turn the peg until the wobble stops.

Tuning tips

  • Tune up to the note. If a string is sharp, loosen it below the note and bring it back up. It holds pitch better that way.
  • Go round twice. Start on the low E and work across. Changing one string's tension pulls the neck a little, so check all six again at the end.
  • One string at a time. Pluck firmly, let it ring, and keep the others quiet. The needle settles after a moment.
  • No sound on an iPhone? Turn off silent mode.
